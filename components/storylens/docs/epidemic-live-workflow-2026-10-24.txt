Assistant and timeline orchestration: verified client code
SendConversationMessage returns conversationId, inputTurnId and replyTurnId.
ConversationTurnSubscription streams TextPart, ThinkingPart, NextStepsPart,
RecordingListPart, SoundEffectListPart, ErrorPart, TimelineChangedPart and
TurnCompletionPart. These are typed client messages, NOT recovered private
assistant function-tool definitions. TimelineChangedPart announces fromVersion
and toVersion. The workspace adopts remote accepted operations and waits for
the required revision. Chat completion and saved timeline completion are
separate states. Cancellation, resumption, event deduplication and stored
conversation identity are present in the selected source.

The live network window observed successful GraphQL event-stream responses
on client-api.epidemicsound.com/soundtrack-graph/graphql/, a saved timeline GET
and the timeline live-update stream. The captured request shape contained
conversationId and turnId. Console observations showed timeline versions
advancing from 2 to 3 to 4 and four tracks loaded. Earlier 30-second stream
reconnects and HTTP/2 errors did not prevent the eventual arrangement.
Passive REA captures retain value-free payload shapes, not raw private request
or response bodies. Do not invent a hidden prompt, model invocation or ranking
score from those shapes. Initial upload/earlier turn traffic was not captured.

Actual exposed operations and their boundaries
SearchRecordings: music retrieval.
RecordingWithSimilar: similar-music candidates.
SearchSoundEffects: SFX retrieval.
SoundEffectAnchors: source audio hitpoint groups/segments.
MusicTypeahead and SoundEffectTypeahead: search suggestions.
SuggestVideoTimeline, VideoTimeline and VideoTimelineSubscription: timed plan
creation/recovery. This older route and the current chat route coexist in code;
their presence does not prove this E7 chat used every operation.
VoiceoverGenerate, GetVoiceoverStatus and VoiceoverDownload: a separate voice
feature. CARTESIA and ELEVENLABS appear as voiceover provider enums. They are
NOT evidence for the soundtrack planner or Vision provider.
Video annotation channels distinguish event windows, cuts, hit instants and
per-clip audio anchors. The producer label soundtracking-agents/video-analysis
does not disclose a model vendor. Flags such as agents_vision_model and
agents_music are configuration switches, not recovered model identities.

Tools/technology identified with the correct evidence scope
1. Search AI: Epidemic's 16 September engineering disclosure identifies AuroLA,
   based on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, for production SFX search. Audio and metadata are
   embedded offline; text queries are embedded online. Elasticsearch fuses
   lexical and semantic KNN results. SGLang serves the embedding encoder.
   A separate binary reranker is described, but invocation on every E7 query
   and the exact production checkpoint remain unknown. Publicly documented
   deployment is not runtime proof of the exact internal request in this video.
   https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/state-of-the-art-audio-retrieval/
2. Editor: React/TanStack client modules, typed GraphQL operations, streamed
   conversation events and versioned saved timeline operations.
3. Arrangement: separate timeline start, source excerpt offset, duration,
   candidate references and gain/fade settings. Overlap opens additional lanes.
   Sound starts and simultaneous layers are different measurements.
4. Audio: Web Audio API, independent clip sources/gain/fades, channel and role
   buses, optional dialogue-keyed music/SFX sidechain and a master limiter.
   The inspected editor enables its limiter. Sidechain has separate optional
   engagement; presence does not prove ducking was enabled on E7. There is no
   verified every-overlap automatic EQ/pan/reverb optimizer in inspected code.
5. Media/export infrastructure: Mediabunny, encoder references, browser offline
   audio processing, zip.js packaging, and server-generated native timeline
   export jobs. These are not the sound-selection AI. Exact bundled package
   versions and successful native E7 export were not established.

Mix graph supported by previous exact client inspection
source -> clip gain/fade/mute -> channel processing/mute/solo -> role bus
(music, SFX, dialogue) -> summed master processing/limiter -> output.
Suggested section timings and source hitpoints are distinct. A source event at
h lands at timeline_start + h - source_offset. The client scheduling contract
supports this; the private detector/selector for h remains unknown.

Storylens implementation implications (our design, not recovered source)
Use an explicit per-video creative brief; separate music, event spotting,
retrieval, excerpt/hitpoint selection, arrangement and mixing. Use distinct
semantic, emotional and measured visual triggers, not four repeated sound
families or a density quota. Keep short scene-level planning batches with
their actual local timing evidence, then check the complete arrangement for
cross-scene consistency. Ask only genuinely missing creative choices; preserve
owner defaults instead of forcing a questionnaire every time.
Keep cue/event identities, alternatives, source offsets, rejections and owner
edits across preview/rebuild/export. Require persisted revision and playback
evidence rather than treating a friendly assistant completion as success.
Use local/credited assets and existing typed Storylens tools. Internal Epidemic
endpoints are evidence, not supported integration APIs to copy into Storylens.
Public AuroLA release licensing and resource requirements remain unresolved;
no model download, weight training or complete private AI recovery occurred.
