Epidemic Sound: public model and research evidence
Inspected: 24 October 2026. Primary sources only; read-only research.

RESULT
Publicly released audio retrieval and reranking weights DO exist from the
Oxford/Epidemic collaboration: AuroLA. Epidemic explicitly discloses production
use for sound-effects search, which Studio calls. This is a concrete reusable
research lead, not recovery of Studio's complete private video planner.
No model weights, datasets or software were installed or executed in this work.

1. Production disclosure: AuroLA retrieval
Source: https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/state-of-the-art-audio-retrieval/
Date displayed: 16 September 2026; authors ML Team and Jilan Xu.
Observed: AuroLA uses Qwen2.5-Omni-7B's language model, audio encoder and
projector. The post explicitly says it powers production sound-effects search.
Audio and tag embeddings are computed offline and stored as
aurola_audio_embedding and aurola_tags_embedding in Elasticsearch. Online
queries use the text encoder; lexical matching and semantic KNN are combined
through score fusion. SGLang serves the encoder. Studio analyzes video and
calls this search to obtain appropriate sound effects.
The post describes NLI contrastive warmup, AudioVerse training with HybridNCE,
and a separate LoRA Yes/No reranker. It does not establish that every Studio
request invokes that reranker, nor identify a production checkpoint hash,
video-understanding model, planner prompt, hitpoint model or exact fusion weights.
Deployment statement is the company's disclosure, not our independent runtime
verification. The public research checkpoint is not proven identical to the
currently deployed internal model.

2. Research paper and training data boundary
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18010
PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18010
Authors: Jilan Xu, Carl Thome, Danijela Horak, Weidi Xie, Andrew Zisserman.
Affiliations include Oxford VGG, Epidemic Sound and Shanghai Jiao Tong.
Initial submission: 20 February 2026. Current v2: 5 October 2026, titled
Re-purposing Multimodal Large Language Models for Audio-Text Retrieval.
The repository still uses the earlier Scaling Audio-Text Retrieval title.
The paper describes an MLLM shared audio/text embedding space, contrastive
training and AudioVerse: roughly 1.4 million clips collected from 11 sources.
Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B supplies long, short and tag descriptions; the data spans
AudioSet, Freesound, BBC effects, commercial-source collections and academic
benchmarks. This is not a single permissively licensed stock library.
Paper, blog and release documentation are different revisions; do not assume
their training-objective descriptions or summary-token notation are identical.
Availability of the assembled AudioVerse audio/annotations was NOT established.

3. Public code, training scripts and checkpoint releases
Repository: https://github.com/Jazzcharles/AuroLA
This is the paper authors' implementation. It includes retrieval/rerank demos,
training and evaluation code. The AudioVerse section remains TODO. Its PT
checkpoints exclude AudioCaps, Clotho, VGGSound and EPIC-Sounds; Full uses the
full training mixture. Published benchmark scores are authors' reports.

Fine-tuning instructions:
https://github.com/Jazzcharles/AuroLA/blob/main/docs/finetune.md
These accept user audio files and caption metadata, then train a separate LoRA
adapter using finetune_retrieval.sh and evaluate with test_retrieval.sh. This
documents a genuine weight-training route; merely passing Storylens reference
memory to the existing Qwen planner is not that route.

does not establish the release author's licence for every derived artifact.

4. Exact public inference contract (static code; not executed)
Retrieval demo:
https://github.com/Jazzcharles/AuroLA/blob/main/demo/demo_retrieval.py
Input is a batch of audio paths or text queries represented as Qwen chat
messages. Both modalities request a one-word summary; an assistant message
contains the special marker <emb>. Audio preprocessing passes through
qwen_omni_utils.process_mm_info and AutoProcessor, padding tensor batches.
The loader requests final-layer hidden states. get_embed_feature selects the
position immediately BEFORE <emb>, not mean pooling or a generated caption.
Each resulting vector is L2-normalized. Matrix multiplication of text vectors
by transposed audio vectors produces [N_text, N_audio] similarity scores.
This requires indexing and querying with the same trained checkpoint and
preprocessing; generic Qwen text embeddings cannot simply replace one side.

3B config:
https://huggingface.co/Jazzcharles/AuroLA-3B/blob/main/config.json
text_config.hidden_size=2048 and audio_config.output_dim=2048. Combined with
the demo's selected final text hidden state, the expected retrieval dimension
is 2048 (static inference, not a measured run). This config records BF16 and
Transformers 4.57.1. The 7B dimension was not verified in this inspection.

Reranking demo:
https://github.com/Jazzcharles/AuroLA/blob/main/demo/demo_rerank.py
Input is a batch of joint (audio path, candidate text) pairs. The model receives
an audio query and candidate description, then generates exactly one token with
sampling disabled. rerank_yes_prob softmaxes only the Yes and No token logits
and returns the Yes probability. This is pairwise reranking, not an embedding
index or a timestamp predictor. Apply only after candidate retrieval if adopted.
An audio/video flag in a generic processor does not prove this release has been
trained or validated for video-to-soundtracking or precise hitpoint detection.

5. Public Soundly benchmark: evaluation data, restricted scope
Source: https://huggingface.co/datasets/epidemicsound/soundly
Publisher: official Epidemic Sound organization.
The card specifies 1,297 sound-effect records, 6,153 audio files, 82 categories,
human captions/tags/taxonomy and a test split. Multiple files may belong to one
effect record. The advertised size is 8.27 GB. The stated licence is
CC-BY-NC-ND-4.0. This is a released retrieval/Foley evaluation benchmark;
it should not be relabeled an unrestricted commercial sound library or the
AudioVerse training dataset. No data was downloaded here.

6. Official video/audio evaluation research: no deployment inference
Source: https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/soundtracking-difficulties-with-ai/
The current page header shows 5 July 2026; its footer says research first
published 4 May 2026. A 29 April date was not confirmed by this page.
The ML team compares open, internal and commercial soundtracking systems with
silence and randomly swapped audio baselines. Public ImageBind evaluates
semantic audio/video similarity; an AudioSet-trained Synchformer checkpoint
estimates temporal offsets. MMAudio is cited as related generative work.
The post finds these automatic metrics can reward inappropriate soundtracks
and stresses listening/user feedback. It does NOT establish ImageBind,
Synchformer or MMAudio as current Studio production selectors or generators.
Application: measure semantic fit, exact event timing and perceived mix quality
separately. Do not treat a single embedding similarity as acceptance evidence.

7. Adapt: publicly described proprietary music model
Source: https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/conditioning-conflicts-in-adapt/
Date shown: 5 July 2026; author Mauro Luzzatto.
Explicit product link: a proprietary latent diffusion model powers Adapt,
using a VAE/latent DiT and rectified flow, with global text plus time-varying
drums, bass, melody, vocals or instrument stem conditioning. It modifies
artist-opted-in catalogue music. This is distinct from locating library music
or planning Studio's full video soundtrack.
The research studies conflicts between requested style and source stems.
CLAP is used for evaluation; this is not evidence that CLAP is its generator.
The inspected article provides no downloadable Adapt weights or complete
training data. Experimental step/CFG choices must not be presented as universal
production settings. Applicability is conditioning discipline, not local Adapt
installation or a recovered internal model.

8. Historical music similarity research
Source: https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/latebreaking/000012.pdf
Title: Musical Audio Similarity with Self-supervised Convolutional Neural
Networks (ISMIR 2021 late-breaking demo).
Authors Carl Thome, Sebastian Piwell and Oscar Utterback list Epidemic Sound.
They describe audio-example music search using 128-dimensional normalized
ConvNet embeddings, triplet objectives, musical augmentations, and genre/mood
metadata. Training used 40,000 catalogue tracks with track-level splits and
ten sampled ten-second excerpts per track. Five professional video producers
evaluated the search interface. This supports passage-level music similarity
as a long-standing design direction, not proof of today's Studio architecture.
The inspected paper does not link a reusable checkpoint or its proprietary
40,000-track training corpus. Its CC-BY-4.0 paper licence is not a music licence.

9. Practical next steps for Storylens (our recommendations)
1) Keep current working sound retrieval and exports. Evaluate optional AuroLA
   after clarifying release licences and scheduling a realistic hardware check;
   do not download both model sizes and both rerankers merely to connect a tool.
2) Candidate integration: offline same-model sound embeddings plus searchable
   descriptions/tags; query with scene/action/emotion text and combine lexical
   and vector ranks. Preserve chosen source, attribution and rejection state.
3) A pairwise reranker can shortlist plausible effects. Exact timestamps must
   still come from measured editing events, onset/contact evidence and retained
   source offsets. Retrieval relevance is not synchronization.
4) For music, index passages and musical descriptors, not only whole tracks.
   Respect dialogue density and emotional progression, then verify transitions
   and ducking through listening. The 2021 paper motivates this design but does
   not supply a modern production music selector.
5) Evaluate against owner-rated Storylens examples, including a silence option,
   wrong-but-semantically-related sounds, and audible timing/mixing failures.
   Record any result as local evaluation, not equivalence to Epidemic Studio.

UNRESOLVED
Private video model and orchestration; prompt/tool policy; deployed checkpoint
revision; hitpoint detection weights; recommendation fusion coefficients;
catalogue-scale human preference data; complete AudioVerse release rights;
explicit AuroLA release licence; and real performance on this user's GPU.
Those are not recoverable merely by seeing a public client or reading papers.
