WHAT WAS LEARNED

Start with the intended feeling and story arc. Choose this video's sonic
identity before searching for individual files. A wedding, a streetwear promo,
a documentary and a dry comic monologue should not inherit the same generic
UI/bell palette. Consistency means a coherent palette with purposeful variation,
not one sound repeated everywhere. Random variety is also not composition.

Build the emotional foundation. Music, tonal material and voiceover can carry
the narrative together. Melodies can answer gaps in the voice; compatible drones,
chords and textures can prepare a musical arrival. Camera reactions and physical
details support the story where useful. Five categories are not five compulsory
tracks, and neither sparse nor dense effects should become a fixed quota.

Use contrast. Negative space can create anticipation and make a return feel
stronger. Sometimes a music dropout is right; sometimes it is too abrupt and a
filtered reduction preserves momentum better. A volume dip and a frequency
sweep are different choices. Quiet does not always mean digital silence.

Give layers complementary roles. Low weight, mid definition and high detail can
combine into one full gesture. Stacking similar bright sounds does not achieve
that. Keep speech readable by making spectral room in the supporting music,
instead of only turning everything down or making every layer louder.

Treat tonal compatibility as a real choice. Pitched effects can clash with music;
choose compatible material or audition a suitable pitch shift. Higher and lower
registers can change the feeling. Do not copy one fixed semitone direction from
an example or pretend noise-only material has a confidently measured key.

Arrange music. Select useful sections, and use authorized stems to control
instrumentation and avoid competition with the voice. A reversed fragment of an
incoming song can prepare its entry; a controlled last beat/reverb tail can let
the outgoing section settle. Swells or hits can reinforce a specific transition,
but should not become another automatic template on every cut.

Distinguish physical realism from emotional expression. Ambient detail and Foley
need scene context. Creative sounds may intentionally express a subjective
feeling instead of literal reality, but that choice needs a coherent purpose.
One creator's coffee/nature metaphor is deliberate; unrelated birds or horses
on the owner's ordinary speaking video are not justified by that example.
Vision must keep observed physical evidence separate from proposed metaphors.

Shape space and continuity. Panning can support source position and motion.
Reverb can support environment, distance, memory or a purposeful tail. J-cuts
introduce next-scene audio before its picture; L-cuts let previous audio linger.
Filters can suggest a phone, muffling or an underwater perspective when supported
by the story. None of these are universal presets to apply everywhere.

Review the complete sum. Relative balance, speech clarity, clipping/headroom,
the final loudness and playback on different speakers matter. Editable exports
must reconstruct the approved mix. A meter pass is not creative listening approval.
