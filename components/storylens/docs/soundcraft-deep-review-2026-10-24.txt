What SoundCraft actually supplies

It is a Rust digital audio workstation and programmable audio executor. Its
external agent executes commands; the repository does not itself supply the
video-to-sound creative director requested for Storylens. Bounded scans of all
Rust sources and crate manifests found no Ollama/OpenAI/Whisper/Freesound clients,
semantic music catalogue, emotion model, or video/text-to-audio model integration.
This is a bounded source finding at the pinned commit, not a claim about future
releases or every possible external plugin.

1. Actual multitrack layering and mixing
   crates/mix/src/lib.rs:1-6 documents the actual implemented signal flow:
   overlapping clip sources/gain/fades, track trim and inserts, pre/post sends,
   fader/mute automation, pan, buses/aux returns, and master processing. The
   implementation is MixEngine::render at line 641; overlapping clips enter
   process_strip at 927 (clip loop at 963), and render_clip at 1448 adds source
   samples to the destination with per-clip gain. Fades/gain changes are
   interpolated at segment boundaries (gain_at at 1471). Sends are routed at 866
   and receive automation at 1781. render_range at 2070 uses the same engine for
   offline output. No model decides that five layers should be added at any time.

2. Mixing processors, automation, and tails
   crates/dsp/src/plugins/ contains actual EQ, dynamics, reverb, modulation,
   harmonic and utility processors. crates/mix/src/lib.rs:318 and 1194 onward
   implement per-clip EQ/compressor/gain, while track plugins can feed common
   reverb/delay buses. automation lanes live in crates/model/src/automation.rs;
   both volume and plugin/send parameters can be driven from timed points.
   crates/dsp/src/offline.rs:615 exposes plugin-tail-aware processing.
   These are useful ingredients for independent envelopes, designed tails,
   frequency separation and spatial layers, beyond merely summing files.

   Caution: the compressor's "sidechain high-pass" is an internal detector.
   crates/dsp/src/plugins/dynamics.rs:159-184 reads the SAME io channels into
   its detector; this is not evidence of a speech bus automatically ducking a
   separate music bus. No automatic dialogue-keyed mixing policy was found.

3. Actual programmable surface
   docs/control-protocol.md:28 onward defines newline JSON over loopback TCP.
   engine.execute invokes named commands; session.inspect returns tracks,
   timed clips, inserts, sends, routing, automation and source availability.
   Positions accept exact samples or seconds. docs/mcp.md:99 onward and
   crates/automation/src/mcp.rs:24-76 expose command listing, execution,
   batch execution, inspection, import, save and bounce. The adapter dispatch
   at mcp.rs:174 maps bounce_mix to file.bounce_mix. Documentation describes
   a maximum 1,000-command batch; failures remain visible in results.
   crates/automation/tests/it/agent_tasks.rs:66-115 demonstrates sends,
   plugin insertion, volume automation and headless bounce through the API.
   These tests exist upstream; they were not run in this review.

4. Actual timing and video support
   crates/video/src/lib.rs:1-15 supplies movie decoding, video metadata,
   thumbnails, keyframe-to-playhead frame access and supported-codec boundaries.
   This synchronizes a picture track with a DAW timeline; it is not visual
   understanding, object/action recognition or emotion analysis.
   crates/engine/src/io.rs:151 imports audio at sample positions, and :194
   imports a video reference. crates/dsp/src/offline.rs:398 detects acoustic
   transients. Those are timing primitives, not proof a transient is a whoosh,
   punchline, dialogue syllable, or the desired sound-design placement.

5. Actual waveform generation and catalogue boundaries
   crates/dsp/src/plugins/synth.rs:249 onward implements a polyphonic subtractive
   synthesizer; drums.rs:1 and :122 implement a synthesized drum kit. sampler.rs
   plays imported samples. These can make tones/percussion and designed accents,
   but are not MMAudio/FoleyCrafter equivalents and do not create semantically
   grounded Foley from a video. NOTICE:10 onward says demo audio is synthesized
   in code. Downloading this repo does not add a cinematic sound-effect library.

   CLAP in crates/clap-host/src/lib.rs:1-6 means loading .clap audio PLUGINS.
   It is NOT the CLAP text/audio embedding model used by Storylens for retrieval.

6. Actual output and quality measurement
   crates/engine/src/io.rs:376 implements a streaming Bounce with the mix engine;
   Bounced at :397 returns peak, integrated LUFS and true peak (:518-522).
   Per-track stems are produced at :630 onward, with sends/aux included.
   crates/dsp/src/meter.rs:2 and :239 onward implement gated integrated LUFS
   and 4x oversampled true-peak metering. This is more specific metering than
   Storylens's approximate gated RMS. These measurements do not decide whether
   the selected sounds are relevant or creatively satisfying. Normalization
   and limiter/plugin behavior must be configured; automatic mastering or
   speech-driven ducking cannot be inferred merely from processor availability.

7. Licensing and handoff
   Cargo.toml:9 declares MIT OR Apache-2.0. NOTICE retains copyright and terms.
   Fonts/dependencies/assets have separate listed notices. docs/brand/ logos
   are trademark material with separate restrictions; do not import them into
   a Storylens fork. README's status section calls this pre-alpha and lists
   AAF/OMF interchange as missing. Its audio stems could feed existing Storylens
   XML/CapCut handoffs, but a SoundCraft session is not automatically editable
   in Premiere/CapCut. No new integration or native editor check was performed.

Actionable differences for Storylens

Highest priority remains a better sound director: identify narrative/cut/graphic
beats, choose complementary acoustic roles, support offset entrances/contacts/
tails, search multiple precise candidates and abstain from irrelevant sounds
without collapsing the entire composition to isolated word clicks. SoundCraft
does not supply this missing director or a sound library.

Reusable mixer ideas: per-layer envelope and pan curves; shared reverb/delay
returns instead of repeated full-size effects; dedicated dialogue/music/accent/
ambience buses with explicit gain rules; processor-tail handling; integrated
LUFS/true-peak measurements; inspectable render receipts. Keep dialogue ducking
explicit and controlled in Storylens. Retain per-layer stems plus timeline data.

An optional adapter could translate a Storylens typed sound plan into a sequence
of track.new, file.import_audio, clip gain/fade, mix.insert/send, automation points
and bounce calls. Keep it isolated until native execution/format compatibility
is tested. Replacing the existing mixer with a DAW before fixing planning would
not make the four-event output become a good layered soundtrack.
